# Research pipeline

The research pipeline runs a research topic through Slicewright the way a feature runs through it: it agrees the questions with a person, researches them from sources with evidence, agrees the results, files the follow-ups the person chose as issues, and publishes a research page and, when a decision was reached, a decision record. It is this repo's pipeline, in `.slicewright/research.json`, for the people who develop Slicewright; the plugin doesn't ship it. It runs the plugin's [research stage](research-stage.md), and it is loaded as a [named configuration](named-configurations.md) with `--config research`. A research topic then goes through the same gates and leaves the same records as a feature, and later slices start from what it found.

## Requirements

### The file

- `.slicewright/research.json` holds only `pipeline`. Its shared params are the plugin's defaults, so `activeSlicePath` stays `./slice`: there is one slice at a time across both pipelines, and `start-slice.sh` keeps working.
- It is loaded instead of `config.json`, not on top of it, so it repeats what it needs from `config.json`: intake's and release's contexts are the same text in both files.
- It passes the runner's own check of a pipeline, and the static command's `configs` check ([Plugin tests](plugin-tests.md)). No stage's skill is `TBD`.
- It changes nothing in the default pipeline, `.slicewright/config.json`, or in the plugin's `defaults.json`: the research stage isn't in the default pipeline.

### The stages

In order:

1. `intake`: `slice:intake`, `fork`, with the same context as `config.json`'s intake stage: the pre-start checks, the issue as the input, and the slice's branch.
2. `clarify`: `slice:clarify`, `fork`. Its context says how a research topic fills the spec template: the requirements are the research questions, one each, concrete enough that an answer can be judged right or wrong; each acceptance criterion says what a good answer to its question contains, the evidence it needs, and when it is good enough; the scope lists the areas the topic covers and the sources in and out; the constraints hold the rules of evidence for the topic; follow-up slices are later research; what a feature spec has and a research topic doesn't is left out. Its one review agent checks the questions: each has what a good answer contains, the scope covers them all and says which sources are in and out, none is answered by the project's documentation or code already, none holds two questions, and every open question has a recommended answer.
3. `questions-review`: `slice:human`, `inline`, with `spec.md` and `input.md` as its files and no choices. It presents the questions, the scope with the sources, and what a good answer contains, with a summary of clarify's latest entry in `design-reviews.md`; walks the person through the open questions and the assumptions; applies the answers to `spec.md`, keeping its format and removing answered questions; and the person decides whether to approve. On approval it appends a `## Session summary` to `input.md`, as the default pipeline's spec-review does.
4. `research`: `slice:research`, `fork`, with `onError` going to `results-review`, so that a failure is put to the person. Its context names the project's own knowledge beyond the documentation: the plugin's code under `plugins/slice`, this repo's configurations, and the records of earlier slices in `.slicewright/slices/`. It gives the order of trust, from the project's own code and documentation, through the primary source for a matter and a secondary source that reports it, to a vendor's marketing; says that a source the spec rules out isn't read; says how to cite, a page as its link with the date it was read, `<link> (read <YYYY-MM-DD>)`, and a file as its path with the line or section; and says what counts as evidence: what the cited source says, as a short quote or a fact taken from it, or what the agent checked itself, with the command or step and what it showed. A summary from memory, or a claim without a source, isn't evidence. Its research agents: `web`, a `general-purpose` agent that searches the web and reads the primary sources it finds, never only a search result's summary; and `claude-code`, a `claude-code-guide` agent for what concerns Claude Code, its plugins, skills, hooks, subagents and settings, the Claude Agent SDK and the Claude API, which answers nothing found for anything else. Its one review agent checks `research.md` for an answer without evidence, or a claim whose cited source doesn't say it, opening the source; a source the spec rules out, or a question with no answer; evidence that disagrees without the disagreement noted; a confidence the evidence doesn't justify; an option without trade-offs; a recommendation that doesn't follow from the conclusions; an open question without a recommended answer.
5. `results-review`: `slice:human`, `inline`, with `research.md` and `input.md` as its files, and four choices: `accept` goes to `file-issues`, `more-research` back to `research`, `re-scope` back to `clarify`, and `drop` ends the run. When the latest research row in `status.md` is failed, it says so first, with the reason from the log, says that `accept` needs a finished research, so that only the other three apply, and says that a failure in the spec itself, `NO_SPEC` or `SPEC_NOT_READY`, is fixed through `re-scope`, not `more-research`; with no `research.md`, it presents only the failure. Otherwise it presents `research.md` in its own words: each answer with its confidence, the conclusions, the options, the recommendation and the follow-ups, with a summary of research's latest entry in `design-reviews.md`. It walks the person through the open questions; for each one answered, it applies the answer where the question says it affects, keeps the format, removes the question from Open questions, and adds one line under `## Decisions`, creating that section after Recommendation when it is missing. It asks for the decision on the recommendation and writes it on its `Decision:` line, in the person's words, or `none: <why>`. For each follow-up it asks on its own whether to file it as an issue, and writes `Decision: file` or `Decision: no issue`. Then the person chooses: `accept`, only when the recommendation and every follow-up have a decision; `more-research`, after each request is written as one line under `## Review notes` in `research.md`, naming the question or section it concerns; `re-scope`, after a `## Re-scope` section with the reasons and the new direction is appended to `input.md`, never changing the text above it; or `drop`.
6. `file-issues`: `slice:deploy`, `fork`. For each follow-up in `research.md` whose `Decision:` is `file`, it looks for an issue of `prosoftsolutions-cloud/slicewright` with the same title, open or closed, with `gh issue list`, keeping only an exact title match, and when there is none creates one with `gh issue create`: the follow-up's title, and a body with its text, why it matters, and where it comes from, the slice's title and, when `input.md` has one, its source issue. The target is the repository on GitHub, the endpoint is the list of issue URLs, created or found, each with its follow-up's id, and the check is `gh issue view` on each. With no follow-up marked `file`, it records that there was nothing to file, with no endpoint, and returns `ok`. It runs before `consolidate`, so that the research page and the archived records carry the issue numbers.
7. `consolidate`: `slice:consolidate`, `fork`, with `./.slicewright/slices` as its archive and three knowledge agents:
   - `research` writes the research page `architecture/research/<name>.md`, where `<name>` is the slice's title from `status.md` in lowercase, its words, runs of letters and digits, joined with `-`, the way consolidate names a records folder for a slice without an id, so that a later consolidation finds the same page and updates it. The page's frontmatter has `type: research`, the date from `created` in `status.md`, and the source issue when there is one. It holds the questions with their answers, evidence and confidence, the disagreements, the conclusions, the options, the recommendation with the decision taken, where a `Decision:` that starts with `none` means none was taken, shown with its reason, the decisions from the reviews, the open questions, and the follow-ups, each with the link of its issue from the latest entry in `deployments.md` whose endpoint lists issue URLs, or "not filed".
   - `adr` writes one decision record in `architecture/decisions/` when the recommendation's `Decision:` line is filled and doesn't start with `none`: numbered after the last, in the form of the records there, with the context from the conclusions, the decision as the person wrote it, the other options as the alternatives with why each lost, the consequences, and a link to the research page. A record the decision replaces is marked superseded, and the two are linked. Otherwise it writes nothing.
   - `index`, with the default pipeline's index context plus one rule: a folder whose `index.md` is new is listed under Contents in its parent folder's `index.md`. So `architecture/research/index.md` lists the page, `architecture/index.md` lists the folder, and `log.md` gets the slice's entry.
8. `release`: `slice:deploy`, `fork`, with the same context as `config.json`'s release stage: push the branch, and open or update the pull request against `master`.

The pipeline always has a person at its gates, `questions-review` and `results-review`, so it never runs headless.

### Permissions

`.claude/settings.json` allows `Skill(slice:research)`, and the commands the pipeline runs in `file-issues` and in intake: `Bash(gh issue create:*)`, `Bash(gh issue list:*)` and `Bash(gh issue view:*)`. `/slice:configure --config research --permissions` proposes the same rules, from its rule for every stage's skill and for the commands a `slice:deploy` context names, and no `Skill(...)` rule for a skill the pipeline doesn't use.

### Starting and continuing it

- `sh start-slice.sh --config research <issue number>` prepares the working copy and starts the session with `/slice:new --config research <issue number>`; without an issue number, with `/slice:new --config research`, so that the runner asks for the request. The script checks that `.slicewright/research.json` exists before Claude starts.
- A research slice in progress is continued like any other: `/slice:resume`, or `/slice:new` with **Continue**, both switch to `research.json` from the `config:` line of `slice/status.md`.
- The README documents the pipeline as its example of `--config`: what it is for, its stages in order, that it lives in `.slicewright/research.json`, how it starts, and that `/slice:configure --config research --permissions` proposes its permission rules.

### What a run leaves

- `slice/status.md` with `config: research.json`, and after the last stage `status: done` and an empty `next`.
- The research page under `doc/architecture/research/`, listed in that folder's `index.md`, in `architecture/index.md` and in `log.md`.
- A decision record exactly when the person wrote a decision other than `none`.
- One issue for each follow-up marked `file`, and none for the others.
- The slice's records in `.slicewright/slices/`, with an index entry, and a pull request for the branch.

## Acceptance criteria

- Given `.claude/settings.json`, when it is compared with the version before the pipeline was added, then the only change is that `permissions.allow` gained `Skill(slice:research)`, `Bash(gh issue create:*)`, `Bash(gh issue list:*)` and `Bash(gh issue view:*)`.
- Given `.slicewright/research.json`, when `/slice:new --config research` starts in this repo, then the runner reports no pipeline problem and names no stage whose skill is `TBD`.
- Given the file, when it is read, then its stages are exactly the eight above, in that order, each with the skill, mode, `next`, `onError`, `files`, `choices` and agents given above; it has no `sharedParams`; and intake's and release's contexts equal `config.json`'s.
- Given the working copy, when the static command runs, then its `configs` check passes on `research.json`.
- Given a research topic filed as an issue of this repository, when `sh start-slice.sh --config research <issue>` runs and the person answers each gate, then the run passes through every stage in order; `slice/status.md` ends with `status: done` and an empty `next`; the research page exists under `doc/architecture/research/` and is listed in that folder's `index.md`, in `architecture/index.md` and in `log.md`; a decision record exists exactly when the person wrote a decision other than `none`; one issue exists for each follow-up marked `file` and none for the others; the slice's records are in `.slicewright/slices/` with an index entry; and the branch has a pull request.
- Given that run, when its research stage's transcript is read, then both research agents, `web` and `claude-code`, were started at once, and each at most twice.
- Given `.slicewright/research.json` in this repo, when `/slice:configure --config research --permissions` runs, then the rules it proposes include the four rules above, include no `Skill(...)` rule for a skill the pipeline doesn't use, and `.slicewright/config.json` is unchanged.
- Given the README, when a reviewer reads its Recipes, then the research pipeline is the example of `--config`, and every stage name, choice, path and command in it matches `research.json`, the skill and the script.

## Limitations

- The pipeline hasn't run end to end yet. The smoke test runs only the default pipeline, and the test stage of the slice that built the research pipeline ran only the smoke suite, so nothing has yet shown a research topic going through the eight stages and leaving the research page, the decision record and the issues. Reason: a headless test of the research pipeline was left out of scope, because the pipeline always has a person at its gates; its only end-to-end check is a hand run, left to the first research topic after the merge. Until then, the file is proven only as far as the runner's check and the static `configs` check go.
- Intake's fix message, when its pre-start checks fail, says to quit and run `sh start-slice.sh`, without `--config`. Intake is the first stage, and `status.md` exists only after it succeeds, so nothing records the configuration yet, and following the message starts the default pipeline for the research topic. The person has to add `--config research` themselves. Reason: the context must equal `config.json`'s, and the code review rated the message a suggestion; the slice shipped it unchanged: the round had no blocking finding, so no review decision was taken. The fix it proposed: make the sentence configuration-neutral in both files, such as "run `sh start-slice.sh` again, with the options you used".
- The README's recipe names the second choice of `results-review` "more research", where the choice the person types, and the key in `research.json`, is `more-research`; the other three match. Reason: the code review rated it a suggestion, and the slice shipped it unchanged: the round had no blocking finding, so no review decision was taken.
